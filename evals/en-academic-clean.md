Prior work has examined the effect of batch size on generalization in stochastic gradient descent. Keskar et al. (2017) reported that large-batch training tends to converge to sharp minima, which correlate with a larger generalization gap. However, subsequent studies (Hoffer et al., 2017; Goyal et al., 2017) showed that much of this gap can be closed by adjusting the learning rate schedule and training for more iterations. In this paper, we revisit the question using a controlled setup in which the number of parameter updates is held constant across batch sizes. Our results suggest that, under this constraint, the generalization gap is substantially smaller than previously reported, although it does not disappear entirely for batch sizes above 8,192.
